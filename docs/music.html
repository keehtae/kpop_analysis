Music

The popularity scores and other song metadata comes from Circle Chart, South Korea's national music chart. Specifically, the data comes from the digital chart which tracks domestic song popularity based on an aggregate of streaming, downloads, BGM (background music sales), and V coloring (video ringback tone usage). The information was scraped and loaded into a bronze layer table. Then the rows were filtered to only include Kpop groups.

For the sake of this analysis, a Kpop group is closely defined with idol culture in Korea. Choreography and fan engagement should be key components of these groups. As a result, groups such as 2am or Day6 are not included because they focus more on vocals and do not contain any dance elements. Additionally, sub-units of a Kpop group are not included. Any artist who leaves a Kpop group or maintains a separate solo artist career is not included; however, solos included as songs within albums from a Kpop group are included. For example, Up by Karina is a solo, but it was included in Synk: Parallel Line, an album released by the Kpop group Aespa. Moreoever, any song with a featuring is removed as well as any song containing a name of a producer in the title. Having a featured artist or specifying the producer may garner more popularity or curiousity from a broader audience. But for the sake of my analysis, I hope to limit popularity as much as possible to the audio characteristics of a group's song. Therefore, any external factors which may impact the popularity of a song is removed.

Limitations and Future Direction

Social media, marketing, and album sales are not included as variables to help account for the popularity of a song. Future direction of this project hopes to integrate these elements to better isolate the impact of a song's audio characteristic on popularity.
